# Finance & Accounting Office Work: Task Types and Playbooks

Scope: AP, AR/collections, expense management, month-end close/reconciliation, vendor master data, treasury/payments, tax/compliance admin; MNC GBS/shared services down to small businesses. Research conducted 2026-10-02. Source dates noted inline. Where a figure comes from a vendor blog or an unverified secondary summary, it is flagged.

## 1. Canonical process taxonomies and the concrete "work item" level task types

### Takeaway
APQC's PCF "Manage Financial Resources" category gives a stable, cross-industry taxonomy (revenue accounting incl. credit, billing, AR, collections, deductions; general accounting & close; AP & expense reimbursement; treasury; internal controls; tax). Industry practice regroups these into procure-to-pay (P2P), order-to-cash (O2C) and record-to-report (R2R). The work items that actually arrive in a queue sit one or two levels below the PCF "process" level (e.g. "payment status inquiry", "bank change request", "short payment / deduction", "unmatched invoice").

### Cited Findings
- APQC PCF (definitions doc v2.0.0, Nov 2012, built on PCF 6.0.0; category numbered 8.0 there, 9.0 in PCF 7.x) "Manage Financial Resources" process groups: Perform planning and management accounting; Perform revenue accounting; Perform general accounting and reporting; Manage fixed-asset project accounting; Process payroll; Process accounts payable and expense reimbursements; Manage treasury operations; Manage internal controls; Manage taxes; Manage international funds/consolidation. KPIs at category level: finance FTEs per $1B revenue, total finance cost as % of revenue. — [APQC 8.0 Manage Financial Resources Definitions and Key Measures (2012)](https://www.apqc.org/sites/default/files/K04087_8.0_Manage_Financial_Resources.pdf)
- Revenue accounting sub-processes as defined by APQC: Process customer credit (credit checks on new orders/customers, new account applications, review of existing accounts); Invoice customer (master files, billing data transmission, posting receivables, **resolution of customer billing inquiries**); Process AR (receipt/deposit of payments, **application of cash remittances**, posting to GL); Manage and process collections ("generating letters and phone calls on delinquent accounts, deciding when to collect, and selecting a collection vehicle (e.g., an external agency or an in-house department)", negotiating, processing adjustments, writing off uncollectible balances); Manage and process adjustments/deductions (warranties, claims, returns, recalls; correspondence and negotiation with customers; chargeback invoices). — [APQC (2012)](https://www.apqc.org/sites/default/files/K04087_8.0_Manage_Financial_Resources.pdf)
- General accounting per APQC: maintain chart of accounts, process journal entries, allocations, period-end adjustments (accruals, currency conversion), intercompany transactions, reconciliation of GL and intercompany accounts, trial balance, management adjustments. — [APQC (2012)](https://www.apqc.org/sites/default/files/K04087_8.0_Manage_Financial_Resources.pdf)
- Expense reimbursement per APQC: approval and processing of advances and reimbursements, capturing tax data, managing personal accounts; "often dovetails with processing accounts payable". Treasury "Manage cash": reconcile cash positions, oversee EFTs, cash-flow forecasts, bank relationships, resolve/confirm bank fees. Tax "Process taxes": accounting for taxes collected or paid. — [APQC (2012)](https://www.apqc.org/sites/default/files/K04087_8.0_Manage_Financial_Resources.pdf)
- In the current PCF (7.x), AP activities include 9.6.1.1 Verify AP pay file with PO vendor master file; 9.6.1.2 Maintain/manage electronic commerce; 9.6.1.3 Audit invoices and key data in AP system; 9.6.1.4 Approve payments; 9.2.2 Invoice customer includes resolving customer billing inquiries (search-result summary of PCF 7.x documents; not fetched directly). — [APQC PCF Cross-Industry 7.4 (via IFRC copy)](https://solutions.ifrc.org/sites/default/files/2024-10/K014750_APQC%20Process%20Classification%20Framework%20(PCF)%20-%20Cross%20Industry%20-%20PDF%20Version%207.4.pdf)
- Work-item-level AP inbox task types named in a 2026 product launch (AppZen AP Inbox Service Center, Apr 21 2026): payment status inquiries, bank change requests, duplicate invoice resolution, vendor statement reconciliation, W-9 submissions, remittance requests; eight prebuilt agents doing "email interpretation through ERP record lookup to policy evaluation and structured response generation". AppZen claims AP reviewers spend "as much as one week per month" on this (vendor claim). — [PR Newswire / AppZen, Apr 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/appzen-launches-ap-inbox-service-center-bringing-ai-agents-to-the-most-manual-process-in-accounts-payable-302738537.html)
- Hackett (Nov 2025) lists AI-assisted AP areas in leading solutions: automatic invoice classification and inbox management, regional tax policy interpretation, risk scoring/fraud prevention, "approver intelligence". — [The Hackett Group, Nov 19 2025](https://www.thehackettgroup.com/hackett-identifies-ap-solutions-delivering-gains-through-ai/)
- Month-end close activities ranked by how often teams name them as most time-consuming (Ledge 2025, n=100): (1) reconciling accounts (banks, credit cards, payment processors); (2) accruals and provisions; (3) data hygiene (corrections, reallocations, reclasses); (4) variance / budget-vs-actual analysis; (5) departmental submissions and approvals. — [Ledge, Month-end close benchmarks 2025](https://www.ledge.co/content/month-end-close-benchmarks-for-2025)

## 2. What an AP/AR inbox actually contains, and exceptions vs straight-through

### Takeaway
Email is the dominant channel for both invoices and supplier inquiries. About half of invoices still arrive non-electronically. The average AP shop processes only about a third of invoices straight-through and has an exception rate around 18%. Staff spend about 22% of their time on supplier inquiries, dominated by "has / when will my invoice be paid". On the AR side, unapplied cash, missing remittance, short payments and disputes are the work. Bank-change emails are a major fraud vector, so the inbox carries adversarial content.

### Inferences
- In a typical mid-maturity AP shop, roughly 1 in 5 invoices is an exception and roughly 2 in 3 need some human touch (35% straight-through). The human-handled residue is the natural fit for an "AI pre-processes, human decides" pattern: the exception reason, PO/GR lookups and the vendor/requester to chase can be assembled before the human sees the task.
- An AP shared mailbox mixes at least three classes that need different handling: (a) documents to process (invoices, credit notes, statements, W-9s), (b) inquiries needing a lookup and reply (status, remittance), (c) high-risk change requests (bank details). Class (c) must never be auto-actioned from the email itself, because the fraud surveys show the email channel is attacker-controlled.
- Supplier inquiries are mostly caused by exceptions and approval delays (Ardent links the two). Resolving the underlying blocked invoice removes the inquiry, so linking "inquiry" tasks to their underlying "exception" task matters.

### Gaps
- No rigorous public breakdown of AR inbox composition (remittances vs disputes vs invoice-copy requests vs promises-to-pay) was found. Most AR stats come from vendors (HighRadius, Emagia, Versapay).
- IOFM survey data on exception causes ranked by frequency was not found in accessible form.

## 3. What documented SOPs/playbooks look like (exceptions, 3-way match, collections ladders, approval matrices, bank changes)

### Takeaway
Finance playbooks are highly standardized, deterministic decision trees with a few judgment gates: match tolerance and variance routing in AP; time-boxed dunning ladders (reminder at 1-30 days, call plus account-manager copy at 31-60, AR-manager escalation and credit hold at 61-90, final demand/agency at 90+) in AR; maker-checker and dual approval in close and payments; and an "independent-contact callback" rule for bank-detail changes. The judgment gates (write-offs, credit holds, dispute validity, approvals above thresholds, bank changes) are where SoD and audit trail keep humans in the loop.

### Cited Findings
- Collections escalation ladder (practitioner/vendor guides): Stage 1 (1-30 days) assume clerical error, gentle reminder. Stage 2 (31-60) add phone contact and copy the customer's account manager. Stage 3 (61-90) escalate to the AR manager with explicit consequences (late fees, credit hold, service suspension). Stage 4 (90+) formal final demand referencing all prior contacts, then a third-party agency. Most B2B companies send 4-6 communications over 60-90 days before third-party collections. At 90+ days past due, the likelihood of collection reportedly drops below 50%. — [Resolve, B2B dunning letter templates](https://resolvepay.com/blog/b2b-dunning-letter-templates); [Gaviti, when to send to collections](https://gaviti.com/when-to-send-unpaid-invoices-to-collections/); [Kognitos, automating collections: the bottleneck is exceptions](https://www.kognitos.com/blog/automating-collections-and-dunning/)
- APQC's collections process itself includes "deciding when to collect", "selecting a collection vehicle (e.g., an external agency or an in-house department)", "holds internal discussions around account resolution", and writing off uncollectible balances, which marks these as decision points. — [APQC (2012)](https://www.apqc.org/sites/default/files/K04087_8.0_Manage_Financial_Resources.pdf)
- Bank-detail change SOP ("independent-contact rule"): no banking change takes effect until someone independently phones the vendor. Verify through contact information you already had from a different source (vendor master, signed contract, previously verified invoice, official website), never the number, email, link or portal in the change request. Use a script that asks the caller to state the details rather than reading them out, and log the verification. — [Trustpair, call-back procedures](https://trustpair.com/blog/call-back-procedures/); [CallbackProof, independent-contact rule](https://callbackproof.com/resources/the-independent-contact-rule-explained); [Stampli, vendor bank change callback protocol](https://www.stampli.com/resources/vendor-bank-change-callback-protocol/)
- AFP 2025's fraud case shows the failure mode the SOP guards against: a spoofed email, AP not following controls when changing payee bank data, then funds wired to fraudsters. — [AFP 2025 (Truist PDF)](https://www.truist.com/content/dam/truist-bank/us/en/documents/info/cci/2025-afp-payments-fraud-control-survey-report-key-highlights.pdf)
- Match-exception SOP content (university controller guidance and vendor guides): classify by variance type; for missing receipt, route to the requester/receiver to enter the GR; for price variance outside tolerance, route to the buyer to amend the PO or to the vendor for a credit note; for no/invalid PO, return to the vendor or route to the requester. — [Ohio State Univ. Controller](https://busfin.osu.edu/university-business/controller/tips-and-tricks/match-exceptions-tips-and-tricks); [Tellius, why invoices fail three-way match](https://www.tellius.com/resources/blog/blocked-invoices-three-way-match-linkage)
- Ardent 2025 notes that automated routing and approval workflows process invoices "straight-through" when they meet configured business rules, and route exceptions to people. 70% of AP departments have automated routing/approval workflow in use. — [Ardent 2025 (PDF)](https://d15fjz85703yz4.cloudfront.net/1517/5157/1685/Ardent_Partners_-_State_of_ePayables_2025_-_Bottomline_-_FINAL.pdf)
- Close playbooks run on a checklist with dependencies. 56% of teams cite dependencies on other departments/regions as the top close bottleneck, 50% managing processes in Excel, 40% legacy systems, 37% understaffing. 94% use spreadsheets during close. — [Ledge 2025](https://www.ledge.co/content/month-end-close-benchmarks-for-2025)
- AppZen (Apr 2026) describes agent playbooks as: interpret email, look up the ERP record, evaluate policy, generate a structured response, with each decision "auditable, showing what the agent evaluated and why it acted". — [PR Newswire / AppZen](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/appzen-launches-ap-inbox-service-center-bringing-ai-agents-to-the-most-manual-process-in-accounts-payable-302738537.html)

### Inferences
- Finance playbooks map cleanly to "branch on category, then gather context, then decide AI vs human". Sub-cases within a type are well defined: price vs quantity vs missing-GR exceptions; days-past-due tiers; deduction reason codes. This favours a declarative rule with AI classification at the branch and deterministic steps after it.
- The decision gates that must stay human fall into recurring categories: (1) anything changing who gets paid (bank details, new vendor); (2) money out above a threshold (approval matrix / delegation of authority); (3) writing off or crediting customer balances, and credit holds (customer relationship and P&L judgment); (4) sign-off on journal entries and reconciliations (maker-checker). AI pre-processing (lookup, evidence bundle, draft reply, draft JE) is acceptable before each gate.
- Escalation ladders are time-driven, not event-driven. A task tool needs scheduled re-evaluation (re-open a collections task at day 31/61/91) as well as inbound-event triggers.

### Gaps
- I found no public, vendor-neutral example of a full approval matrix / delegation-of-authority table with actual thresholds for a named company. Thresholds vary by company and are rarely published.
- No neutral survey quantifies the typical tolerance thresholds used in 3-way match (e.g. ±2% / $X). Practitioner guides mention tolerances but not distributions.

## 4. What consultancy/analyst studies say is automated, automatable, or stubbornly manual

### Takeaway
Rule-based, high-volume steps (T&E, payment scheduling, invoice approvals routing, payment execution, data capture) are the most automated. Exception management, supplier onboarding, cash management and analytics are the least. Only ~4% of AP teams have fully automated exception management. Leading AP platforms reach ~60% touchless (Hackett 2025); the average shop is ~35% straight-through (Ardent 2025). AI adoption in finance plateaued around 58-59% (Gartner 2024-2025), with AP automation the #2 use case. Close is <40% automated for most teams and Excel-bound.

- Deloitte 2023 GSS survey (~500 organizations, 6 industries): automation remained the most desired digital enabler. CFOs are the most common C-suite owner of GBS. Cost reduction is the primary objective, with relocation to lower-cost locations. — [PR Newswire, Deloitte 2023](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/deloittes-global-shared-services-and-outsourcing-survey-2023-leading-global-business-services-gbs-organizations-align-closely-with-c-suite-objectives-301906515.html)
- Close (Ledge 2025, n=100, companies with 51 to 10,000+ employees): close takes 1-3 days for 18% of teams, 4-5 days for 32%, 6-7 days for 23%, >7 days for 27%. Most automate <40% of the close. Cash reconciliation takes 20-50 hours/month using 3-5 systems; blockers are missing payment details, mismatched amounts and fragmented sources. — [Ledge 2025](https://www.ledge.co/content/month-end-close-benchmarks-for-2025); [CFO.com coverage](https://www.cfo.com/news/50-of-finance-take-week-to-close-books-ledge-month-end-close-time-cfo-three-day-close-myth-/746085/)
- Expense reports (GBTA Foundation / HRS, Nov 2015, global; older than the 2019 preference but still the industry-cited benchmark): **$58** and 20 minutes to process a report; **19%** contain errors or missing information; each correction adds 18 minutes and $52. Top pain points: attaching receipts (37%), entering data (33%). 70% of companies process internally with third-party software; Europe was more paper-based (35%). — [GBTA, Nov 2015](https://gbta.org/new-study-reveals-pain-points-in-expense-reporting/)
- Ardent 2025 cost history: in the early 2000s, cost per invoice exceeded $20 and cycle times exceeded 20 days; both are now "less than half". — [Ardent 2025 (PDF)](https://d15fjz85703yz4.cloudfront.net/1517/5157/1685/Ardent_Partners_-_State_of_ePayables_2025_-_Bottomline_-_FINAL.pdf)

### Inferences
- The "stubbornly manual" residue is consistent across sources: exception management, supplier onboarding/master data, reconciliation break investigation, disputes and deductions, and anything needing information from another department or the counterparty. That residue is unstructured, email-borne and needs cross-system context gathering, which matches Jidoka's niche (classify, gather context via MCP, hand to human) better than core straight-through processing, which ERPs/AP suites already do.
- Hackett's O2C finding that collectors spend less time "gathering information... and preparing for calls" is the same pre-processing pattern (prepare, then hand off) Jidoka's rules support.
- Treat AI-driven projections (Hackett AI World Class) as modelled targets, not observed outcomes.

### Gaps
- APQC open-standards benchmark medians (e.g. cost per invoice line, invoices per AP FTE) are behind a login. Only the measure definitions were found ([APQC measure page](https://www.apqc.org/resources/benchmarking/open-standards-benchmarking/measures/total-cost-perform-process-process-10)).
- McKinsey, EY, PwC and KPMG finance-function automation estimates (e.g. % of finance activities automatable) were not retrieved within the tool budget.
- No 2019+ independent replacement for the GBTA 2015 expense-report cost benchmark was found. Many vendor sites recycle the $58 figure.

## 5. How small businesses handle the same work

### Takeaway
In small businesses the "playbook" lives in the owner's or part-time bookkeeper's head and inbox. Work arrives as emailed bills, receipts and bank feeds into QuickBooks/Xero. The dominant pain points are chasing customers for payment (AR) and, for outsourced bookkeepers and accounting firms, chasing clients for missing documents. There is little segregation of duties: one person often enters, approves and pays. Volumes are low, but each item is interrupt-driven and context-poor.

### Cited Findings
- QuickBooks 2025 US Small Business Late Payments Report (Jan 2025, n=2,487 US businesses with 0-100 employees): **56%** are owed money from unpaid invoices, averaging **$17,500**; **47%** have invoices overdue 30+ days; nearly 1 in 10 invoices is overdue on average. Affected businesses had lower accounting-software adoption (36% vs 56%) and more credit reliance. — [Intuit QuickBooks, 2025 Late Payments Report](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/r/small-business-data/small-business-late-payments-report-2025/)
- QuickBooks midsize-business payments research (as summarized in search results; page not fetched): 65% of businesses spent on average **14 hours/week** on admin related to collecting payments. — [QuickBooks midsize payments research](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/r/midsize-business/midsize-payments-research/)
- Intuit QuickBooks Business Solutions survey (Aug 2024, firms with 10-99 employees): respondents report on average **25 hours/week** on manual data entry or reconciling data across apps. — [Intuit, Business Solutions survey 2024](https://erp.intuit.com/blog/research/business-solutions-survey-2024/)
- Xero: with bookkeeping software, most small business owners spend 1-3 hours/week on bookkeeping. Small businesses report saving an average of 5 hours/week on bills with Xero (vendor claim). — [Xero, small business bookkeeping guide](https://www.xero.com/ph/guides/small-business-bookkeeping/)
- Financial Cents State of Accounting Workflow 2024 (n=367 accounting/bookkeeping firm owners): the #1 workflow challenge is "getting information and documents from clients" (**65.2%**, up from 53.8%). Before automation, 52.4% received client documents only after several days. The most wanted features are a centralized dashboard (73.9%), client reminders (69.3%) and a client portal (60.6%). The 2025 edition (n=816, mostly North America) found document collection is still the top issue. — [Financial Cents 2024 report](https://financial-cents.com/resources/articles/2024-report-state-of-accounting-workflow-automation/); [Financial Cents 2025 report](https://financial-cents.com/resources/articles/2025-report-state-of-accounting-workflow-and-automation/)
- Vendor-content estimates for firms (low confidence): a firm with 80 monthly clients sends 240-320 document requests per month, each needing ~2.3 follow-ups; 40-60% of clients miss the first document deadline. — [Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/blog/accounting-reduce-document-collection-time/)
- AFP 2025: organizations with revenue under $1B reported higher fraud incidence than larger ones (83% vs 73%), suggesting smaller organizations with fewer controls are at least as exposed. — [AFP 2025 (Truist PDF)](https://www.truist.com/content/dam/truist-bank/us/en/documents/info/cci/2025-afp-payments-fraud-control-survey-report-key-highlights.pdf)

### Inferences
- MNC GBS vs small business differ mainly in (a) volume and specialization (dedicated AP inquiry desks, ERP exception queues, tiered dunning teams vs one person's inbox), (b) controls (formal SoD, approval matrices, maker-checker vs owner approves everything), and (c) channel (portals/e-invoicing networks/ERP queues vs email, photos of receipts, bank feeds). Task types are largely the same. Small firms just lack exception queues, so everything lands in email.
- For small firms and their outsourced bookkeepers, the highest-value task types are "chase customer for payment" (AR dunning), "chase client for missing receipt/statement" (document collection), "categorize unrecognized bank-feed transaction" and "bill received, then enter and schedule payment". All of these are email-heavy and suit AI drafting plus human send.
- For an accounting/bookkeeping firm serving many clients, the "task source" is effectively many client inboxes or a shared practice inbox. Task types are then per-client-engagement (monthly close for client X, missing docs for client Y), a different shape from in-house AP.

### Gaps
- No reliable source found on the share of small businesses using an external bookkeeper vs the owner doing the books vs an in-house bookkeeper (US or global).
- Tax/compliance admin (sales-tax/VAT notices, 1099/W-9 collection, payroll tax notices) task volumes and handling were not covered by any benchmark found within the tool budget. Only AppZen's mention of W-9 submissions as an AP inbox category was found.
- Treasury work-item data (payment-run approvals, bank-fee disputes, cash-positioning) beyond the fraud/controls angle was not found in benchmark form.
