# Consultancy, Analyst and Academic Views on Automating Office/Knowledge Work (AI, RPA, Agentic AI)

Research date: 2026-10-02. Sources span 2012 (one baseline email stat) and 2019-2026; most are 2023-2026. Labels used below: **[Survey]** = self-reported executive/worker opinion; **[Measured]** = field experiment, telemetry or benchmark; **[Prediction]** = analyst forecast; **[Vendor]** = source has a commercial stake in the conclusion.

## (a) Task-level decomposition of office work and automation potential by activity type

### Takeaway
Every major framework (McKinsey MGI, OpenAI/Penn "GPTs are GPTs", Stanford WORKBank, Anthropic Economic Index) analyses automation at the **task/activity level, not the job level**, and finds that generative AI's main new reach is language-heavy activities (communicating, documenting, applying expertise), which is exactly the inbound-email/ticket work Jidoka targets. Partial automation of many tasks is the norm; full automation of whole jobs is rare.

### Cited Findings
- [Prediction/Model] McKinsey Global Institute (June 2023): current GenAI plus other technologies could automate work activities absorbing **60-70% of employees' time**, up from ~50% in prior estimates; the jump is largely because GenAI understands natural language, which is required for activities accounting for **25% of total work time**; impact skews to higher-wage knowledge work — [McKinsey, The economic potential of generative AI](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/the-economic-potential-of-generative-ai-the-next-productivity-frontier)
- [Prediction/Model] Same report: half of today's work activities could be automated between **2030 and 2060 (midpoint 2045)**, ~a decade earlier than before; 63 GenAI use cases across 16 functions worth **$2.6-4.4 trillion/year**; ~75% of value concentrated in customer operations, marketing & sales, software engineering and R&D — [McKinsey report PDF](https://www.mckinsey.de/~/media/mckinsey/locations/europe%20and%20middle%20east/deutschland/news/presse/2023/2023-06-14%20mgi%20genai%20report%2023/the-economic-potential-of-generative-ai-the-next-productivity-frontier-vf.pdf) (the 75%/4-functions figure is from the PDF; verify page)
- [Model/Exposure estimate] Eloundou, Manning, Mishkin, Rock ("GPTs are GPTs", Science vol. 384, 2024; preprint March 2023): ~**80% of the US workforce** could have at least 10% of tasks affected by LLMs; ~**19%** could see at least 50% of tasks affected; higher-income jobs more exposed — [ResearchGate (Science version)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381581667_GPTs_are_GPTs_Labor_market_impact_potential_of_LLMs); [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.10130)
- [Survey + expert rating] Stanford SALT lab WORKBank (June 2025): 1,500 domain workers and AI experts rated **844 tasks across 104 occupations**. Workers want AI-agent automation for **46.1% of tasks** (mainly low-value, repetitive ones). Introduces a **Human Agency Scale H1-H5** (H1 = agent does it alone; H3 = equal partnership). **H3 is the dominant worker-desired level in 45.2% of occupations**; on **47.5% of tasks** workers want more human agency than experts judge technically necessary — [arXiv 2506.06576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06576); [project site](https://futureofwork.saltlab.stanford.edu)
- [Measured, usage telemetry] Anthropic Economic Index: share of "directive" (fully delegated) conversations rose from **27% (late 2024) to 39%** (Sept 2025 report); enterprise **API traffic is 77% automation** vs ~50% for consumer Claude.ai — [Anthropic, Economic Index Sept 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-september-2025-report)
- [Measured] Anthropic Economic Index Jan 2026 report (Nov 2025 data): on Claude.ai augmentation rose back to **52%**, automation fell to **45%**; Office & Administrative Support tasks rose to **13%** of transcripts (email management, document processing, CRM, scheduling) — [Anthropic, Economic primitives Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report) (figures as summarised by search results; verify against report)
- [Measured] The original Feb 2025 Economic Index launch reported roughly **57% augmentation vs 43% automation** of Claude.ai conversations — [Anthropic, Introducing the Anthropic Economic Index](https://www.anthropic.com/news/the-anthropic-economic-index) (figure from memory of the launch post; confirm)
- [Survey] Asana Anatomy of Work Index (10,000+ knowledge workers; 2021/2022 editions): **~60% of time on "work about work"** (communicating about work, searching for information, switching apps, chasing status), ~26-27% on skilled work, ~13% on strategic planning; per year 103 h unnecessary meetings, 209 h duplicative work, 352 h talking about work — [Asana, work about work](https://asana.com/resources/why-work-about-work-is-bad); [Asana 2022 press release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220405005399/en/5182748/Asana-Anatomy-of-Work-Index-2022-Work-About-Work-Hampering-Organizational-Agility) **[Vendor: Asana sells work-management software]**

### Inferences
- The analyst consensus that value lies at the *task* level, with sub-tasks of a job split between AI and humans, directly supports Jidoka's unit of work (one inbound item = one task, typed and routed individually) rather than whole-process or whole-role automation.
- WORKBank's H1-H5 scale is a near-perfect external vocabulary for Jidoka's per-type rule outcome (assign to AI vs. "pre-process then human" vs. human). Jidoka currently has a binary AI/human assignment plus pre-processing; WORKBank suggests the middle (H2-H3, "AI prepares, human decides/co-works") is where most worker demand sits.
- Enterprise API usage skewing to automation (77%) while consumer/chat usage stays augmentative suggests that once a workflow is codified (as Jidoka's rules are), organisations push toward delegation — so Jidoka's rules should be expected to drift from "prepare for human" to "AI handles" over time per type; versioned rules fit that trajectory.

### Gaps
- No source found giving a clean breakdown of *inbound* work (email/tickets) by type-share across functions (e.g., % that is routine/standard vs exception). McKinsey's activity taxonomy is economy-wide, not inbox-specific.
- McKinsey's 60-70% is *technical* potential, not adoption; no firm measured equivalent found.

## (b) State of agentic AI adoption in enterprises 2024-2026 and where it works

### Takeaway
Adoption claims are high but scaled production use is low: roughly a quarter of firms scale agents anywhere, ≤10% in any given function, and Gartner predicts >40% of agentic projects will be cancelled by end-2027. Measured agent benchmarks on realistic office tasks show 30-58% success, collapsing on multi-turn/communication-heavy work. Agents work best in IT ops, software engineering and narrow, well-instrumented workflows.

### Cited Findings
- [Survey] McKinsey State of AI 2025 (Nov 2025): **88%** of organisations use AI regularly in ≥1 function (78% a year earlier), but only ~**one-third** have begun scaling AI; **23%** are scaling AI agents somewhere and a further **39%** are experimenting; in any single business function **no more than 10%** report scaling agents — [McKinsey, The state of AI in 2025](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai); [Forbes summary, Mar 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/josipamajic/2026/03/22/10-of-enterprise-functions-use-ai-agents-mckinsey-finds/)
- [Survey] McKinsey State of AI 2025: AI high performers (~6% of respondents) are ~**3x as likely** to have fundamentally redesigned workflows (55% vs much lower for others) and are more likely to have **defined processes for when model outputs need human validation** — [McKinsey State of AI](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai); [CX Today summary](https://www.cxtoday.com/ai-automation-in-cx/mckinseys-state-of-ai-the-scaling-gap-is-now-cxs-problem/) (the ~6% high-performer share is from the McKinsey report; the full page timed out on fetch, so verify)
- [Survey] McKinsey "Superagency in the workplace" (Jan 2025): **92%** of companies plan to raise AI investment over 3 years, but only **1%** call themselves mature; employees are 3x more likely than leaders think to expect AI to do ≥30% of their work within a year — [McKinsey Superagency PDF](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/quantumblack/our%20insights/superagency%20in%20the%20workplace%20empowering%20people%20to%20unlock%20ais%20full%20potential%20at%20work/superagency-in-the-workplace-empowering-people-to-unlock-ais-full-potential-v4.pdf?); [Lewis Silkin summary](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/our-thinking/future-of-work-hub/insights/2025/01/28/superagency-in-the-workplace-empowering-people-to-unlock-ais-full-potential)
- [Prediction] Gartner (25 June 2025): **over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end of 2027** due to escalating costs, unclear business value or inadequate risk controls; most current projects are hype-driven PoCs, "often misapplied"; "agent washing" — only **~130 of thousands** of agentic vendors are real. - [Prediction] Gartner: **≥15% of day-to-day work decisions** made autonomously by agentic AI by 2028 (from 0% in 2024); **33% of enterprise software apps** will include agentic AI by 2028 (from <1% in 2024) — [WFTV/Gartner](https://www.wftv.com/news/local/agentic-ai-will-handle-15-work-decisions-by-2028-gartner-predicts/VCD3YBGQK5CUBNBMCIKUW6TI5I/); [MES Computing](https://www.mescomputing.com/news/ai/5-predictions-about-agentic-ai-from-gartner)

Tasks needing **chat with colleagues (RocketChat) and office web UIs (OwnCloud)** were hardest; GitLab/project-tracker tasks easiest — [NeurIPS paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/0d744742f6fac4d1134c019b7cef3c8a-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.14161v1)
- [Measured benchmark] Salesforce CRMArena-Pro (May/June 2025): leading agents **58% success single-turn, ~35% multi-turn** on 19 expert-validated CRM tasks; **near-zero confidentiality awareness**; "workflow execution" was the most tractable skill (>83% single-turn) — [arXiv 2505.18878](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.18878); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/salesforce-study-reveals-enterprise-ai-agents-fail-65-of-multiturn-tasks/) **[Vendor-run, but self-critical]**
- [Survey] Microsoft Work Trend Index 2025 (31,000 workers, 31 countries, Feb-Mar 2025): 82% of leaders expect to use "digital labor" within 12-18 months; frames three phases (assistant → digital colleague → humans set direction and agents run processes "with human oversight as needed"); warns of a task-specific **"human-agent ratio"** — too many agents overwhelm human oversight capacity — [Microsoft WTI 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/2025-the-year-the-frontier-firm-is-born) **[Vendor: Microsoft sells Copilot/agents]**

### Inferences
- Gartner's explicit split (agents for decisions, deterministic automation for routine workflows, assistants for retrieval) matches Jidoka's "rule built once, executed deterministically, AI steps the only non-deterministic part" design closely. Jidoka is effectively a deterministic workflow with bounded AI decision points — the profile Gartner says survives the cancellation wave.
- The benchmark data (30% on TheAgentCompany, 35% multi-turn on CRMArena-Pro, worst on chat/office-UI tasks) argues against letting an open-ended agent own end-to-end handling of email-originated work; "workflow execution" scoring >83% supports constrained, playbook-driven execution.
- HFS's "learning loop" critique is a challenge to Jidoka: rules are built once and only change when a user edits the description. Analysts expect value to come from feedback (outcomes, human corrections) flowing back into the automation. Jidoka's saved triage examples are a partial loop for classification, but nothing analogous exists yet for rule quality.
- BCG's "70% of value in core business" plus McKinsey's "≤10% per function" suggests a horizontal triage tool will see value concentrated in a handful of high-volume task types; per-type onboarding should be prioritised by volume.

### Gaps
- No independent, measured (non-survey) data found on what share of *production* agent deployments are fully autonomous vs human-gated.
- IDC and Everest Group adoption numbers for agentic AI were not retrieved (their reports are paywalled; only vendor press about PEAK Matrix placements surfaced).
- Conflicting adoption numbers (KPMG 42% deployed, PwC 79% "adopting", McKinsey 23% scaling, Deloitte 23% moderate use) reflect different definitions and samples; none is directly comparable.

## (c) Human-in-the-loop / "AI prepares, human decides" vs full autonomy

### Takeaway
Measured field studies show the biggest reliable gains when AI assists a human inside their workflow (customer support +14-15%, consultants +25% speed/+40% quality inside the AI's competence), but also that unchecked reliance on AI outside its competence makes outcomes *worse* (-19 pp). Survey data consistently show organisations keep high-stakes and financial decisions human, and that high performers formalise *when* humans must validate output.

### Cited Findings
- [Measured, field study] Brynjolfsson, Li, Raymond, "Generative AI at Work" (QJE vol. 140(2), May 2025; NBER WP 31161, Apr 2023): staggered rollout of an AI assistant to **5,172 customer-support agents** raised issues resolved per hour by **~15%** on average (14% in the NBER version); gains concentrated in less experienced/lower-skilled workers, with small quality *declines* for top performers; AI "prepares" suggested responses that humans choose to use — [QJE](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658); [NBER WP](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31161/revisions/w31161.rev0.pdf). The NBER version reports a **34%** gain for novice/low-skilled workers (from memory of the paper; verify in PDF).
- [Measured, field experiment] Dell'Acqua et al. (HBS/BCG, Sept 2023), 758 BCG consultants using GPT-4: for tasks **inside the "jagged frontier"**, +12.2% tasks completed, **25.1% faster, >40% higher quality**; for a task **outside the frontier**, AI users were **19 percentage points less likely** to be correct — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374015542_Navigating_the_Jagged_Technological_Frontier_Field_Experimental_Evidence_of_the_Effects_of_AI_on_Knowledge_Worker_Productivity_and_Quality)
- [Survey] Stanford WORKBank (June 2025): workers prefer **H3 equal partnership** as dominant level in 45.2% of occupations; want more human agency than experts deem necessary on 47.5% of tasks — [arXiv 2506.06576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06576)
- [Survey] PwC (May 2025): only **20%** of execs trust agents with financial transactions vs 38% for data analysis — [TechMonitor](https://www.techmonitor.ai/ai-and-automation/88-of-us-firms-increase-ai-budgets-agentic-ai-adoption/)
- [Survey] Microsoft WTI 2025: **78% of leaders vs 66% of employees** trust AI for high-stakes work; only 36% of leaders / 21% of employees expect to manage agents — [Microsoft WTI 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/2025-the-year-the-frontier-firm-is-born)
- [Survey] McKinsey State of AI 2025: high performers more likely to have **human-in-the-loop rules and defined processes for when outputs need human validation** — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai)
- [Analyst] Gartner Hype Cycle 2025: organisations show "lack of true trust in AI agents' ability to operate without human oversight" — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-08-05-gartner-hype-cycle-identifies-top-ai-innovations-in-2025)
- [Survey/Analyst] Deloitte 2026: high-impact agentic workflows have **agents handling routine decisions while escalating exceptions to human judgment**; organisations must define "where humans should remain in control" — [Deloitte State of AI 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-shared/docs/about/2025/state-of-ai-2026-global.pdf)
- [Practitioner guidance] Anthropic "Building effective agents" (Dec 2024): distinguishes **workflows** (LLMs and tools orchestrated through predefined code paths) from **agents** (LLM directs its own process); recommends the simplest solution and adding agency only when needed, since agents trade latency/cost/compounding errors for flexibility — [Anthropic resources PDF](https://resources.anthropic.com/hubfs/Building%20Effective%20AI%20Agents-%20Architecture%20Patterns%20and%20Implementation%20Frameworks.pdf); [summary](https://www.aihero.dev/building-effective-agents) **[Vendor]**

### Inferences
- The strongest *measured* evidence (Brynjolfsson; Dell'Acqua) is for AI-assisting-humans, which is Jidoka's "pre-process before handoff" path. The design choice to make pre-processing distinct from full AI execution is well supported.
- The jagged-frontier result implies the per-type routing decision matters more than model quality: the same model helps on in-frontier task types and hurts on out-of-frontier ones. Jidoka's per-type rule is exactly the place to encode "this type is inside the frontier → AI; this type is outside → human with context".
- The trust data (20% for financial transactions) supports Jidoka's write-tool approval gate at rule activation and suggests per-action approval for financial/external-communication tools.
- Novice-skewed gains suggest the "AI summarises + gathers context, then human" pattern is most valuable for less-experienced handlers.

### Gaps
- No large measured study found comparing *the same* task type under "AI drafts, human approves" vs "AI acts autonomously" in an enterprise back-office setting (error rates, throughput).
- Little quantitative evidence on automation bias / rubber-stamping in approval-gated workflows in office settings (as opposed to aviation/medicine).

## (d) RPA / IPA lessons: failure rates, exceptions, standardisation, variants, bot maintenance

### Takeaway
A decade of RPA showed that the technology was rarely the limiting factor: **process fragmentation/non-standardisation** is the top scaling barrier year after year, early projects failed at 30-50%, very few firms scaled beyond ~50 bots, and maintenance consumes a large share of lifetime cost. Process mining reveals that "one process" is in reality hundreds of thousands of variants.

### Cited Findings
- [Survey/Advisory] EY (2016 "Get ready for robots", commonly cited as 2019): **30-50% of initial RPA projects fail**; causes include targeting the wrong processes, treating RPA as an IT project, assuming robots are the whole solution, and pilot skills not scaling — [EY PDF](https://eyfs.ie/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/ey-get-ready-for-robots.pdf); [Forbes, The Big RPA Bubble, 2018](https://www.forbes.com/sites/cognitiveworld/2018/12/02/the-big-rpa-bubble/). Note: secondary sources date the stat to 2019; the EY PDF is from Nov 2016.
- [Survey] Deloitte Global RPA/Intelligent Automation surveys: only **3% scaled** (>50 bots) in 2017/2018 → **4%** in 2018 → **13%** by 2020 — [Legal IT Insider on 2018 survey](https://legaltechnology.com/deloitte-rpa-report-admits-surprise-at-lack-of-vision-and-it-readiness/); [Automation Today on 2020 survey](https://automationtoday.net/news/report-covid-forces-more-organizations-to-scale-up-rpa-and-intelligent-automation/)
- [Survey] Deloitte Global Intelligent Automation Survey 2022 (479 execs, 35 countries): 74% implementing RPA; expected **31% cost reduction over 3 years**; payback for pilots lengthened to **22 months (from 16 in 2020)**; **process fragmentation remains the #1 barrier** (consistent across years), then lack of vision (41% lack an enterprise strategy) and IT readiness; 62% find integrating multiple solutions hard; >50% hadn't calculated actual cost reductions; self-rated maturity **5.04/10**; Deloitte recommends end-to-end automation and process mining for standardisation — [Deloitte Insights 2022](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/talent/intelligent-automation-2022-survey-results.html)
- [Case study, Vendor] Celonis/Siemens: Siemens' Order-to-Cash process had **~300 million activities and ~923,000 process variants**; process mining raised automation rate by 24% and cut rework 11%, ~10 million fewer manual touches/year — [Celonis Siemens story](https://www.celonis.com/solutions/stories/siemens-digital-transformation-process-mining) **[Vendor]**
- [Industry commentary] Large-enterprise P2P processes typically show hundreds to thousands of variants — [AIMultiple, Celonis use cases](https://research.aimultiple.com/celonis/) (secondary)
- [Analyst prediction, 2021] Gartner: by 2024 organisations would lower operational costs **30%** by combining hyperautomation with **redesigned operational processes** (note: tied to redesign, not tech alone) — [Gartner press release Apr 2021](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2021-04-28-gartner-forecasts-worldwide-hyperautomation-enabling-software-market-to-reach-nearly-600-billion-by-2022). No follow-up validating the 30% outcome was found.
- [Low-quality / Vendor blogs] RPA maintenance is commonly claimed at **40-60% of lifecycle effort**, ~40% of 3-year TCO, or 15-25% of build cost annually, driven by bots breaking on UI changes — [Coasty blog](https://coasty.ai/blog/the-hidden-maintenance-cost-of-rpa-bots-nobody-budgets-for); [Codewave](https://codewave.com/feeds/blog/rpa-price). **These come from vendors selling RPA replacements and could not be traced to a primary study; treat as directional only.**
- [Analyst] Forrester (Sept 2025, Bernhard Schaffrik): "Deterministic workflow engines, RPA bots, and DPA tools are not powerful enough to implement the complexities required for autonomous operations"; defines **Adaptive Process Orchestration (APO)** as platforms using AI agents and **nondeterministic control flows in addition to traditional deterministic control flows**, with governance as a core capability — [Forrester blog](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/announcing-the-evaluation-of-the-adaptive-process-orchestration-market/); [Process Excellence Network](https://www.processexcellencenetwork.com/automation/news/forrester-identifies-adaptive-process-orchestration-as-the-future-of-enterprise-automation)
- [Analyst] HFS: "process debt" is one of four debts 85% of leaders say limit AI value — [HFS](https://www.hfsresearch.com/research/four-enterprise-debts-ai-future/)

### Inferences
- RPA failed largely because rules were brittle against variance and exceptions. Jidoka's approach differs in two ways that address this: (1) classification is AI-driven and can absorb unforeseen variety (and propose new types), and (2) per-type rules contain AI steps and branches that handle sub-case variance. This is essentially Forrester's APO pattern (deterministic skeleton + nondeterministic steps).
- But the RPA lesson "process fragmentation is the top barrier" still applies: if users' natural-language handling descriptions are themselves inconsistent, the generated rule encodes that. Jidoka's onboarding could surface variants (e.g., showing several past tasks of the type) before the user writes the description — analogous to process mining before automation.
- RPA maintenance pain came from UI coupling; Jidoka's use of MCP (API-level tools) avoids UI-scraping brittleness, though MCP server/tool schema changes are a new maintenance surface that versioned rules should detect.
- The near-million-variant Siemens figure warns that a "task type" registry could explode or, conversely, hide large intra-type variance; the triage near-duplicate problem (an open decision in Jidoka) is the same problem process-mining tools face with variant explosion.

### Gaps
- No primary, independent study quantifying RPA exception rates (share of cases falling out to humans) was found; figures circulating online are vendor marketing.
- No primary source found for the oft-quoted "maintenance = X% of RPA cost"; flagged above.
- Everest Group's and HFS's RPA-era failure data are behind paywalls.

## (e) Governance needs: audit trails, approvals, segregation of duties, explainability, EU AI Act

### Takeaway
Governance is now the stated #1 blocker for agentic scale (Deloitte ~70% cite trust/governance; only 21% have mature agent governance; Forrester predicts an agent-caused breach). The EU AI Act formalises human oversight (Art. 14), deployer monitoring and **≥6-month log retention** (Art. 26) for high-risk systems. Declarative, versioned, inspectable rules with audit trails are what both regulators and analysts ask for.

### Cited Findings
- [Survey] Deloitte 2026: only **21%** have a mature governance model for autonomous agents; ~**70%** cite inability to trust/govern agents as a major barrier; organisations need to define where humans stay in control, how automated decisions are audited, and which records are retained — [Deloitte State of AI 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-shared/docs/about/2025/state-of-ai-2026-global.pdf)
- [Prediction] Gartner: "inadequate risk controls" is one of three reasons >40% of agentic projects will be cancelled — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)
- [Prediction] Forrester 2026: agentic deployment will cause a disclosed breach leading to dismissals; **63%** of organisations lack AI governance policies (as reported) — [Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/forrester-agentic-ai-breach-2026/)
- [Measured] CRMArena-Pro: agents showed **near-zero confidentiality awareness** — [arXiv 2505.18878](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.18878)
- [Regulation] EU AI Act Article 14: high-risk AI systems must be designed so natural persons can effectively oversee them, including the ability to **intervene or stop** the system — [regulation-ai.eu Art. 14](https://www.regulation-ai.eu/en/articles/article-14/)
- [Regulation] EU AI Act Article 26 (deployers): use per instructions, assign **trained personnel with authority** for human oversight, monitor operation, manage input data, **retain automatically generated logs ≥6 months**, report risks/incidents — [EU AI Act Service Desk Art. 26](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-26)
- [Regulation, timing] High-risk obligations were originally applicable from **2 Aug 2026**; sources report that for most **Annex III** high-risk categories full applicability has moved to **2 Dec 2027** (EU "digital omnibus" delay) — [regulation-ai.eu](https://www.regulation-ai.eu/en/articles/article-14/); [aiacto.eu](https://www.aiacto.eu/en/blog/ai-act-what-changes-august-2-2026). **Sources conflict on exact dates; verify against the Official Journal.** Note Annex III high-risk categories relevant to office work include employment/worker management (e.g., task allocation, performance monitoring) and access to essential services (credit, benefits).
- [Survey] PwC: lack of trust in agents a top-3 challenge for 28% — [Techstrong.ai](https://techstrong.ai/agentic-ai/ai-agents-are-gaining-traction-in-enterprises-but-with-some-hiccups-pwc-survey/)
- [Analyst] HFS execution model calls for explicit **decision rights** and determining where humans remain in workflows — [HFS](https://www.hfsresearch.com/research/agentic-ai-learning-automation/)

### Inferences
- Jidoka's declarative, versioned, user-inspectable rules + a write-tool approval gate + per-task state changes centralised in one orchestrator align with what Deloitte/Gartner describe as missing in most deployments. Ensuring every AI step's inputs/outputs and every tool call are logged per task and retained (≥6 months if ever used in a high-risk context) would map onto Art. 26.
- Jidoka's "task allocation to AI or human" could, if used for allocating work to employees and monitoring them, brush against the EU AI Act's employment-related Annex III category; as a single-user tool it is likely outside scope, but a multi-user version should be checked.
- Segregation of duties (the person who defines a rule shouldn't also be sole approver of its high-risk actions) is not addressed in Jidoka's single-user model; no analyst source found that prescribes this specifically for agents, but it is standard in financial controls.

### Gaps
- No analyst source found specifying segregation-of-duties patterns for AI agents specifically.
- Explainability requirements for AI triage/classification decisions in office workflows are not quantified anywhere found.

## (f) The "unstructured inbound work" problem: email-driven work, IDP, case management

### Takeaway
Inbound communication is a dominant time sink (117 emails/day; interrupted every 2 minutes; 28% of the week on email in older McKinsey data; ~60% "work about work"). The market's response has been intelligent document processing (IDP) with "straight-through processing + exception queues", and case-management/process-orchestration platforms that now blend deterministic flows with AI agents and human-in-the-loop decisioning.

### Cited Findings
- [Measured telemetry + survey] Microsoft WTI 2025 / "Breaking down the infinite workday" (June 2025): during 9-5 workers are **interrupted every 2 minutes** (≈**275 interruptions/day** including off-hours); average worker receives **117 emails/day**, mostly skimmed in under a minute, plus **153 Teams messages/day**; 60% of meetings ad hoc; after-hours chats +15% YoY — [Microsoft, Breaking down the infinite workday](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/breaking-down-infinite-workday); [WTI 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/2025-the-year-the-frontier-firm-is-born) **[Vendor; telemetry is from M365 signals]**
- [Model, 2012 baseline] McKinsey Global Institute "The social economy" (July 2012): "interaction workers" spend **28% of the workweek (~13 h) on email** and **~20% searching for information** or tracking down colleagues — [McKinsey, The social economy](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/technology-media-and-telecommunications/our-insights/the-social-economy)
- [Survey] Asana: 60% of time on work about work, including chasing status and searching for information — [Asana](https://asana.com/resources/why-work-about-work-is-bad) **[Vendor]**
- [Measured] Anthropic Economic Index: Office & Admin Support rose to 13% of Claude.ai transcripts by Nov 2025, including email management and document processing — [Anthropic Jan 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report)
- [Analyst] Everest Group IDP State of the Market 2025 assesses 29 IDP providers plus 10 BFS-specific; market direction is toward LLM/agentic validation that **flags only true exceptions for human review** to raise straight-through processing (STP) — [Everest Group IDP SotM 2025](https://www.everestgrp.com/report/egr-2025-38-r-7283/); [UiPath IDP 2025.10](https://www.uipath.com/blog/product-and-updates/intelligent-document-processing-2025-10-release)
- [Vendor claim] ABBYY claims **99% STP** cited in Everest's 2025 assessment — [ABBYY press](https://www.abbyy.com/company/news/intelligent-document-processing-everest-group-2025/) **[Vendor; best-case, likely on structured document types]**
- [Analyst] Forrester DPA definition includes **dynamic case management** and AI-led support; the newer APO category offers "a unified platform for structured and unstructured work" with **auditability, hybrid execution, and human-in-the-loop decisioning** as key dynamics — [Forrester DPA Wave Q3 2025 (via BP3)](https://www.bp-3.com/hubfs/The%20Forrester%20WaveTM_%20Digital%20Process%20Automation%20Software,%20Q3%202025.pdf); [Flowable on Forrester APO landscape](https://www.flowable.com/forrester-adaptive-orchestration-report) **[Vendor-hosted copies]**
- [Prediction] Gartner: customer service is the canonical inbound-work domain where agentic AI is predicted to resolve 80% of *common* issues by 2029 — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-03-05-gartner-predicts-agentic-ai-will-autonomously-resolve-80-percent-of-common-customer-service-issues-without-human-intervention-by-20290)
- [Measured benchmark] TheAgentCompany: communication-heavy tasks (chatting with colleagues) were the hardest for agents — [NeurIPS paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/0d744742f6fac4d1134c019b7cef3c8a-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf)

### Inferences
- The IDP market's architecture (classify document → extract → validate → STP or exception queue) is structurally the same as Jidoka's (triage → type → rule → AI or human). Jidoka generalises it from documents to any inbound item, and replaces fixed extraction templates with AI-built per-type rules — consistent with where Everest and Forrester say the market is heading.
- Gartner's "80% of *common* issues" framing implies a long tail of uncommon cases stays human; Jidoka's ambiguity → ask-human path and "skip onboarding → human without rule" path are the right shape for the long tail.
- The interruption/email data supports Jidoka's value proposition even with zero automation: triage + summarisation + a single board reduces "work about work" (chasing status, searching for context), which is the 60%/28%/20% bucket.

### Gaps
- No independent measured study found on **email triage time** specifically (time spent classifying/routing inbound mail) as opposed to total email time.
- Gartner/Forrester case-management-specific figures (market size, adoption) are paywalled; only definitions were retrieved.
- No source found quantifying the share of inbound items that are "ambiguous" in type (relevant to Jidoka's ask-the-user rate).

## Cross-cutting: rule/playbook-based vs learned/agentic approaches — does the evidence support Jidoka's design?

### Takeaway
On balance, analyst and academic evidence **supports** Jidoka's core design (AI triage + deterministic, versioned, per-type playbooks with bounded AI steps + human handoff with pre-processing). The main **challenges** are: (1) the lack of a learning loop from outcomes back into rules, (2) process variance/standardisation still being the dominant failure cause, and (3) trust/governance needing finer-grained approvals than activation-time gates.

### Cited Findings
- Gartner recommends agents for decisions, deterministic automation for routine workflows, assistants for retrieval — [Process Excellence Network on Gartner](https://www.processexcellencenetwork.com/ai/news/gartner-almost-half-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-scrapped-by-2028)
- Anthropic recommends predefined-path "workflows" over autonomous agents unless flexibility is needed — [Anthropic PDF](https://resources.anthropic.com/hubfs/Building%20Effective%20AI%20Agents-%20Architecture%20Patterns%20and%20Implementation%20Frameworks.pdf)
- Forrester's APO = deterministic + nondeterministic control flows + governance + HITL — [Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/announcing-the-evaluation-of-the-adaptive-process-orchestration-market/)
- CRMArena-Pro: "workflow execution" >83% success vs 35% for multi-turn tasks — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.18878)
- McKinsey: high performers redesign workflows and define when human validation is required — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai)
- MIT NANDA & HFS: value comes from systems that **learn/adapt** from feedback; static tools stall — [Virtualization Review](https://virtualizationreview.com/articles/2025/08/19/mit-report-finds-most-ai-business-investments-fail-reveals-genai-divide.aspx); [HFS](https://www.hfsresearch.com/research/agentic-ai-learning-automation/)
- Deloitte RPA: process fragmentation #1 barrier for years — [Deloitte 2022](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/talent/intelligent-automation-2022-survey-results.html)

### Inferences
- Supports: build-once/execute-many rules (cheaper, auditable, matches Gartner/Anthropic/Forrester); per-type routing (jagged frontier); pre-process-then-human (strongest measured gains); ask-when-ambiguous triage (trust data); MCP over UI automation (avoids RPA brittleness); versioned declarative rules (governance demands).
- Challenges / suggested design responses: add outcome feedback (human overrides, reassignments, corrections) per type to prompt rule revision — a "learning loop" without per-task regeneration; surface intra-type variance at onboarding; consider graded autonomy levels (H1-H5-like) rather than binary AI/human; per-action approvals for external writes/financial actions; per-task audit log of every AI step and tool call with retention.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found directly comparing playbook-constrained LLM automation vs free-form agents on the same office workload in production; the evidence is triangulated from benchmarks, analyst guidance and surveys.
